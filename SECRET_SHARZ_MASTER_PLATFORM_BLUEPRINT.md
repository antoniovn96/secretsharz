## 1. Executive Definition

Secret Sharz is a social-impact, technology-enabled human-development ecosystem designed to support people across the lifespan. It is not a career-guidance website, counselling website, SEN website, social network, AI application, or collection of disconnected dashboards.

Its purpose is to help people feel safe, understood, supported and capable while giving them access to appropriate human support, knowledge, learning, career direction, opportunities, community, accessibility and lifelong development.

### Emotional promise

**Safe. Understood. Not alone. Able to discover the next step.**

The experience should move people through:

`Safety → Understanding → Support → Self-awareness → Hope → Capability → Empowerment → Connection → Contribution`

### Founder non-negotiables

1. People before profit.
2. Secret Sharz never sells personal data.
3. Secret Sharz must never deviate from helping people.
4. AI assists people and professionals; it does not replace accountable human professionals.
5. Architecture wins over sunk development effort.
6. Vulnerable people must not be exploited for engagement, revenue or data extraction.

## 2. Geographic and Lifespan Strategy

### Geography

`India-first → Global-ready from Day 1 → Global expansion`

Localisation must therefore be architectural, not a late UI addition.

Priority language direction:

`English → Indian languages → International languages`

### Lifespan

Secret Sharz is designed for the entire lifespan. The initial user base may be student-heavy, but the architecture must not be student-centric.

The platform must support age-aware experiences including:

- under 13
- 13–17
- 18+
- adult/lifelong users

Minors cannot independently create accounts. A child/student account is created and supervised by a parent/guardian. At 18, control transitions to the individual through an explicit adult-autonomy process.

## 3. Canonical Platform Model

The central model is:

`ONE PERSON → ONE SECRET SHARZ IDENTITY → MANY LEGITIMATE ROLES/RELATIONSHIPS → SEPARATE PROFESSIONAL DOMAINS → ONE HUMAN JOURNEY`

A person may simultaneously be:

- student
- child
- parent
- professional
- mentor
- volunteer
- researcher
- community member
- institution member
- opportunity seeker

These are relationships and roles, not separate people or disconnected identities.

### Identity separation

- Verified/legal identity is private.
- Display identity may differ from legal identity.
- Community identity may be anonymous/pseudonymous.
- Professional identity is verified.
- Specialist records remain domain-separated.

## 7. Accessibility & Communication Engine

Accessibility is a core institutional pillar, not a polish phase.

The platform must support users with hearing, speech, visual, motor, learning, neurodevelopmental, cognitive and communication differences.

### Planned capability family

`Camera/Microphone → Accessibility & Communication Engine`

Potential capabilities:

- sign/gesture → text
- sign/gesture → speech
- speech → text
- text → speech
- translation
- captioning
- communication assistance
- visual recognition where appropriate
- accessibility preferences
- multilingual communication

The camera/gesture feature must be designed as a reusable engine so future assistive technologies can be added without redesigning the platform.

Privacy requirements:

- explicit camera/microphone permission
- clear disclosure of processing
- prefer on-device processing where feasible
- minimise retention
- no silent recording
- no training on sensitive communication data without explicit approved basis
- accessible controls and fallbacks

## 8. AI Architecture

AI is an institutional capability, not a collection of unrelated chatbots.

### AI layers

#### Layer 1 — Interface guidance

Helps a person understand where they are and how to use a page.

Examples:

- explain an assessment question
- explain a form field
- offer assistance with navigation
- explain what a section measures

#### Layer 2 — Personal guidance

Helps users understand choices and next steps.

Examples:

- career exploration
- learning resources
- reflection prompts
- opportunity discovery
- next-step suggestions

#### Layer 3 — Professional augmentation

Supports professionals with:

- summarisation
- organisation
- resource discovery
- documentation assistance
- administrative support
- pattern surfacing

### AI boundaries

AI must:

- disclose that AI is being used
- explain what information it will use
- access unrelated domains only with explicit approved permission
- never silently ingest sensitive specialist records
- never use sensitive service data for model training without an approved basis
- defer clinical, safeguarding, ethical and legal judgement to humans
- provide escalation to human support
- retain conversations only under an approved retention basis

AI may guide; accountable humans remain responsible.

## 9. Professional Trust Framework

Professionals are part of a multidisciplinary ecosystem.

Verification pipeline:

`Application → Identity Verification → Qualification Verification → Professional Registration Verification → Experience Verification → Background Checks → Secret Sharz Approval → Professional Agreement → Profile Publication`

Professional content initially requires review before publication.

Professional relationships are time-bound and access must be revoked when the relationship ends.

## 10. Parent / Guardian Architecture

A parent/guardian relationship is not equivalent to unrestricted access.

For minors:

`Child → Guardian Relationship → Supervised Account → Service-specific consent/permissions`

Guardian access is service- and safeguarding-aware.

A guardian does not automatically receive:

- counselling notes
- private journals
- psychological records
- private SEN records
- private career information

At 18:

- guardian access is revoked
- adult permissions are established/confirmed
- historic disclosures do not automatically create continuing access

## 12. Authorisation Model

Canonical decision:

`Role + Relationship + Data Domain + Purpose + Consent + Safeguarding + Time/Status = Access decision`

A role alone never grants unrestricted access.

Security must be enforced server-side.

Client-side routing/visibility is UX only.

Privileged role authority should use Firebase Authentication custom claims managed by trusted server tooling.

---

## 13. Consent Model

Consent is:

- granular
- versioned
- service-specific
- auditable
- append-only

Initial categories:

- account/privacy
- counselling
- SEN
- career
- community
- AI-enabled features
- professional publishing
- guardian permissions

Consent withdrawal is separate from account deletion and may not erase records that must be retained under an approved legal/service basis.

---

## 14. Safeguarding

Safeguarding is a dedicated restricted domain.

Safeguarding access is not equivalent to ordinary administrator access.

Break-glass access requires:

- authorised identity
- mandatory reason
- limited scope
- temporary access
- immutable audit event
- post-event review

Secret Sharz is a social-impact organisation.

Working commercial principle:

`Free access to essential discovery/support → paid advanced services where appropriate → subsidised access where needed`

Example career model:

- assessment: free

Potential paid areas may include advanced reports, professional consultations, specialist services, institutional programmes and advanced tools.

Non-negotiable:

Revenue must never override safety, accessibility or human wellbeing.

# 18. CURRENT GITHUB PLATFORM AUDIT — INITIAL BASELINE

## Green — strong foundation already present

### Security foundation

The repository already has a documented three-layer security boundary:

`Firebase Auth → Firestore Rules → Trusted server/admin tooling`

and explicitly treats client-side checks as UX rather than final authorization. The repository also has a role vocabulary, server-managed claims, consent gates, audit events and security tests. 

### Privacy/consent architecture

The repository already defines one person identity across specialist divisions while separating counselling, SEN and career domains. It also defines age/guardian rules, professional relationships, safeguarding, AI boundaries, auditability and accessibility requirements.

### Automated security tests

The repository contains Firestore emulator tests, server tests and Auth/Firestore integration tests for role management and security behaviour.

### Deployment infrastructure

Next.js, Firebase, Firestore and Vercel are already functioning in production. Production Firebase service-account configuration has been repaired and the Admin Overview is currently working.

### Existing specialist functionality

The repository contains meaningful career, college, counsellor, professional and admin functionality. This is valuable implementation inventory and should be preserved where it aligns with the Blueprint.

---

## Yellow — useful but requires architectural alignment

### 1. `users` as a broad directory

The current application uses `users` for multiple profile categories. This is acceptable as a transitional identity/directory layer, but sensitive specialist data must move into protected domains.

### 2. Career case management

Career counsellor case management and roadmaps exist and should be retained, but must be aligned to the canonical `person → relationship → career domain` model.

### 3. Professional directory

The professional-specific directory and creation flow exist. Professional profile data should remain distinct from credential evidence, sensitive case data and authentication authority.

### 4. Existing dashboards

Several dashboards already exist but should be treated as participant/professional experiences on top of shared platform services rather than independent product architectures.

### 5. Legacy data structures

The security foundation documents explicitly identify `students` and `caseFiles` as transitional legacy structures. New sensitive data must not be added to them; dedicated counselling, SEN, career, safeguarding and audit domains are the intended direction.

---

## Red — must be addressed before broad sensitive-data expansion

### 1. Fragmented identity/account model

The platform must converge on one canonical person/account model with relationships and service memberships rather than accumulating product-specific identities.

### 2. Minor/guardian onboarding

The founder decision requires supervised minor accounts. This must be implemented before treating independent student self-registration as the final model.

### 3. Dedicated domain architecture

Counselling, SEN, career, safeguarding, professional verification and other sensitive domains need explicit schemas and tested relationship-aware access boundaries.

### 4. Legacy privileged-role fallback

The repository currently documents a migration-only fallback to `users.role`. Claims should become the sole privileged authority after controlled migration.

### 5. App Check

The repository documents App Check as implemented client-side but not yet enforced. Production enforcement remains a gate for appropriate Firebase services.

### 6. Existing chat proxy

The security foundation documents `/api/chat.js` as a pre-existing unauthenticated wildcard-CORS Anthropic proxy without rate limiting. This requires a dedicated security fix before production use of sensitive AI workflows.

### 7. Consent-before-profile transaction

The security foundation identifies moving consent recording fully in front of profile creation as the next onboarding milestone.

### 8. Dashboard fragmentation

An existing dashboard audit documents competing layouts and orphaned components in the Student Dashboard. This is evidence that dashboard development has already drifted and needs consolidation rather than more parallel dashboard code.

# 19. Implementation Rules Going Forward

1. Do not add a major feature until its domain and shared-service dependencies are defined.
2. Do not create a new identity when a person already exists.
3. Do not put sensitive domain data into broad profile documents.
4. Do not use client-side role checks as authorization.
5. Do not create independent versions of shared platform services inside specialist products.
6. Do not make AI the final authority for clinical, safeguarding, ethical or legal decisions.
7. Do not sell personal data.
8. Do not create unsupervised minor accounts.
9. Do not deploy sensitive-data features without security, consent, safeguarding and accessibility review.
10. Prefer refactoring over preserving contradictory legacy architecture.
11. Every significant implementation must map back to this Blueprint.
12. New feature ideas go into the Master Feature Registry before implementation.

2. Human journey defined.
3. User/stakeholder roles defined.
4. Data domain defined.
5. Source of truth defined.
6. Relationships defined.
7. Consent requirements defined.

10. Accessibility reviewed.
11. AI implications reviewed where relevant.

13. Error/fallback behaviour defined.
14. Tests pass.
15. Production deployment verified.
16. Human review/approval completed for sensitive capabilities.
