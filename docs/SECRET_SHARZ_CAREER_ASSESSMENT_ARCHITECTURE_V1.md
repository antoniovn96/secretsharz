# Secret Sharz — Career Assessment Architecture V1

**Status:** Working Founder product direction — assessment item development and psychometric validation pending  
**Branch:** rebuild/platform-foundation-v1  
**Purpose:** Define the modular online career-assessment ecosystem for students and later adult/professional users.

## 1. Product principle

Secret Sharz should not use one giant test that claims to identify a single "correct career".

The Career Assessment Engine should be modular:

Interests + Aptitudes + Skills + Values + Personality/Work Style + Self-Efficacy + Decision Readiness + Career Adaptability + Context + Career Knowledge
→ integrated interpretation
→ career exploration
→ education/course pathways
→ action plan

Assessment scores are guidance evidence, not destiny, diagnosis or a guaranteed prediction of career success.

## 2. Core assessment families

### A. Career Interests
Primary model:
- RIASEC / Holland interests:
  - Realistic
  - Investigative
  - Artistic
  - Social
  - Enterprising
  - Conventional

RIASEC is a strong foundation for the online platform because O*NET's Interest Profiler is explicitly designed for educational planning, career exploration and career guidance and is based on the RIASEC model. Possible formats:
- Likert interest items;
- activity preference;
- career activity scenarios;
- forced-choice pairs;
- interest card sort.

### B. Aptitude / Ability
Original Secret Sharz assessment modules can measure:
- verbal reasoning;
- numerical reasoning;
- abstract/logical reasoning;
- data interpretation;
- spatial reasoning;
- mechanical/practical reasoning where relevant;
- pattern recognition;
- problem solving;
- attention to detail where relevant.

Aptitude should be presented as performance on assessed tasks, not as a fixed intelligence label.

### C. Skills / Competencies
Assess:
- communication;
- critical thinking;
- problem solving;
- collaboration;
- leadership;
- creativity;
- digital skills;
- research/information literacy;
- planning/organisation;
- presentation;
- numerical/data skills;
- technical/subject-specific skills.

Self-report confidence should be separated from demonstrated performance where possible.

### D. Work Values
Assess preferences such as:
- achievement;
- independence;
- recognition;
- relationships/service;
- support;
- working conditions;
- security;
- flexibility;
- learning;
- impact/purpose.

CareerOneStop's Work Values Matcher demonstrates one established way of connecting work-value preferences with occupational exploration. citeturn815299search0turn815299search1

### E. Personality / Work Style
Use an original, non-diagnostic work-style profile for career exploration, potentially covering:
- social energy;
- structure/preference for planning;
- curiosity/openness;
- persistence;
- cooperation;
- initiative;
- tolerance for ambiguity;
- pace preference.

The system should not turn a personality profile into "you must become X".

Commercial proprietary instruments such as MBTI or Strong Interest Inventory require their own licensing/certification arrangements; Secret Sharz should not reproduce proprietary items or reports without permission. citeturn690371search1turn690371search8

### F. Career Decision Self-Efficacy
Assess confidence in:
- self-appraisal;
- gathering career/education information;
- goal selection;
- planning;
- problem solving.

These are established career-decision competencies measured by the Career Decision Self-Efficacy Scale. The exact CDSE instrument is copyrighted/licensed, so Secret Sharz should develop original items unless it separately licenses the instrument. citeturn815299search3turn815299search4

### G. Career Decision Readiness / Career Maturity
Assess:
- concern/planning;
- involvement;
- decisiveness;
- information seeking;
- consultation;
- confidence;
- readiness to act.

Career maturity is a recognised construct for adolescents and young adults, including the Career Maturity Inventory and related work on career choice readiness. citeturn690371search0turn690371search3

### H. Career Adaptability
Assess:
- concern about the future;
- control;
- curiosity;
- confidence.

These four dimensions are used in the Career Adapt-Abilities Scale research programme. citeturn815299search7

### I. Resilience / Persistence
Secret Sharz can assess career-relevant resilience such as:
- responding to setbacks;
- persistence after rejection;
- recovering from mistakes;
- adapting plans;
- seeking support;
- continuing effort toward long-term goals.

This should be framed as a career-development construct rather than a mental-health diagnosis.

### J. Academic / Subject Fit
Assess:
- subject interests;
- subject confidence;
- demonstrated academic performance where the user provides it;
- preferred learning activities;
- practical/theoretical preference;
- interdisciplinary interests.

Examples:
- mathematics;
- biological sciences;
- physical sciences;
- social sciences;
- humanities/languages;
- commerce/business;
- computing/technology;
- arts/design;
- vocational/practical domains.

This should combine assessment evidence with actual academic records where authorised, rather than treating one test as a substitute for achievement.

### K. Career & Occupational Knowledge
Assess how well the student understands:
- what occupations actually involve;
- required education;
- common entry routes;
- workplace environments;
- advancement;
- realities/trade-offs;
- related occupations.

CareerOneStop's approach separates self-assessment from occupational exploration and uses occupation information to refine career ideas. citeturn815299search2turn815299search6

### L. Career Exploration Behaviour
Assess whether the student:
- researches careers;
- speaks with professionals;
- attends exposure activities;
- compares courses;
- investigates admission requirements;
- tries projects/internships/volunteering;
- reflects on experiences.

This is useful as a progress indicator rather than a fixed trait.

### M. Goal Setting / Career Planning
Assess:
- goal clarity;
- short-term planning;
- long-term planning;
- action sequencing;
- monitoring;
- contingency planning.

### N. Employability / 21st-Century Skills
Assess:
- communication;
- collaboration;
- critical thinking;
- creativity;
- digital fluency;
- adaptability;
- self-management;
- problem solving;
- professionalism;
- responsible technology use.

This can connect directly to Secret Sharz career plans and school programmes.

### O. Entrepreneurship Orientation
An optional assessment for students interested in entrepreneurial pathways:
- opportunity recognition;
- initiative;
- experimentation;
- calculated risk orientation;
- resourcefulness;
- persistence;
- customer/problem orientation;
- tolerance for uncertainty.

It should identify entrepreneurship-related preferences and behaviours, not claim entrepreneurial success is guaranteed.

### P. Digital / AI Career Readiness
A modern optional module can assess:
- digital confidence;
- information evaluation;
- responsible AI use;
- prompt/tool literacy;
- digital collaboration;
- cybersecurity awareness;
- adapting to technology change.

This should be updated as technology and labour-market requirements evolve.

### Q. Work Environment Preferences
Assess preferences such as:
- office / field / hybrid;
- individual / team;
- structured / flexible;
- public-facing / behind-the-scenes;
- fast-paced / steady;
- indoor / outdoor;
- people / data / things / ideas;
- stable / entrepreneurial.

This becomes a useful filter after the core assessment.

### R. Career Narrative / Aspiration
Use open-ended prompts:
- "What do you want your future to look like?"
- "What problems would you like to solve?"
- "What are you proud of?"
- "What kind of work would make you feel useful?"
- "Who or what inspires you?"

AI may summarise themes, but should not convert a narrative into a deterministic career recommendation without the rest of the assessment evidence.

### S. Context and Constraints Profile
Do not score these as personality traits.

Instead capture user-provided planning context such as:
- preferred study location;
- budget range;
- willingness to relocate;
- family responsibilities;
- preferred study duration;
- preferred work location;
- accessibility requirements;
- language preferences;
- institution preferences.

The purpose is to filter realistic pathways, not to label the student.

## 3. Existing Secret Sharz assessment concepts

The existing platform direction already includes:
- RIASEC;
- Aptitude Confidence;
- Career Values;
- Resilience;
- Decision Maturity.

These should become modules inside the larger Career Assessment Engine rather than five unrelated tests.

"Aptitude Confidence" should be kept distinct from actual aptitude performance:
- aptitude = task performance;
- aptitude confidence = perceived ability.

## 4. Recommended assessment products

### Career Explorer
Approx. 10–15 minutes.

Includes:
- interests;
- strengths snapshot;
- aspirations;
- work environment preferences.

Best for early exploration.

### Career Compass
Approx. 25–35 minutes.

Includes:
- RIASEC;
- work values;
- skills;
- work environment preferences;
- strengths.

Best for Grades 6–8 exploration.

### Stream & Subject Compass
Approx. 30–40 minutes.

Includes:
- interests;
- subject interests;
- selected aptitude modules;
- skills;
- values;
- academic context.

Best for Grades 8–10.

### Career Direction Assessment
Approx. 45–60 minutes.

Includes:
- interests;
- aptitude;
- skills;
- values;
- work style;
- decision readiness;
- career self-efficacy.

Best for Grades 9–12.

### Higher Education & Career Planner
Approx. 60–75 minutes, potentially split across sessions.

Includes:
- interests;
- aptitude;
- skills;
- values;
- work style;
- career decision readiness;
- adaptability;
- career knowledge;
- academic/subject fit;
- constraints/preferences.

Best for Grades 11–12 and post-secondary users.

### Career Readiness Assessment
Approx. 20–30 minutes.

Includes:
- employability skills;
- career self-efficacy;
- adaptability;
- planning;
- digital/AI readiness;
- exploration behaviour.

Best for senior students, college students and early-career users.

### Career Transition / Adult Direction
Approx. 30–45 minutes.

Includes:
- interests;
- transferable skills;
- values;
- work environment;
- adaptability;
- career decision confidence;
- goals and constraints.

Best for adults changing jobs, returning to work or considering a new field.

## 5. Mix-and-match engine

Institutions and users should not be locked into one assessment.

A package can be assembled as:

Base:
- Interest

Add:
- Aptitude
- Values
- Skills
- Work Style
- Decision Readiness
- Adaptability
- Academic Fit
- Career Knowledge
- Employability
- Entrepreneurship
- Digital/AI Readiness

Example institutional package:

Grade 9:
RIASEC + Aptitude + Values + Subject Fit

Grade 10:
RIASEC + Aptitude + Values + Decision Readiness + Career Knowledge

Grade 11:
RIASEC + Aptitude + Values + Work Style + Career Decision Self-Efficacy + Academic Fit

Grade 12:
Career Direction + Academic Fit + Career Knowledge + Planning + Decision Readiness

The package engine should allow Secret Sharz to create new assessment combinations without rebuilding the assessment platform.

## 6. Assessment delivery formats

Secret Sharz can use different interaction formats:
- Likert-scale items;
- forced-choice;
- ranking;
- card sorting;
- scenario-based questions;
- situational judgement;
- timed reasoning tasks;
- untimed reflection;
- adaptive testing;
- open-ended responses;
- portfolio/evidence inputs;
- structured achievement data.

The format should be selected according to the construct. A reasoning assessment should not be disguised as a self-report preference questionnaire.

## 7. Reporting architecture

A report should separate:

### What was assessed
Constructs and modules completed.

### What the evidence suggests
Scores, profiles and patterns.

### What to explore
Career families and occupations for further investigation.

### Why the options appeared
Traceable links between assessment evidence and career recommendations.

### What to do next
Courses, subjects, projects, conversations, experiences and skills to explore.

### Confidence and limitations
Explain which evidence is strong, which is self-reported, and where more information is required.

The system should not say:
- "You are destined to be a doctor."
- "You will be successful as an engineer."
- "This is your perfect career."

Instead it should produce ranked or grouped exploration suggestions with transparent evidence, while keeping the user's decision central.

## 8. Career recommendation engine

Career matching should combine multiple evidence layers:

Interest fit
+ Aptitude fit
+ Skill fit
+ Value fit
+ Work-environment fit
+ Academic fit
+ Career knowledge
+ User constraints
+ Current education/pathway
+ labour-market/occupation data
→ exploration set

No single assessment should determine the career list.

## 9. Parent and institution reporting

For students under the applicable age:
- student-facing report;
- parent/guardian-facing report where authorised;
- institution report based on purchased assessment/report scope;
- counsellor/coach professional report where authorised.

Individual assessment reports remain access-controlled and are never automatically visible to all teachers.

Institution dashboards can also receive authorised aggregate insights:
- top interest themes;
- common skill-development needs;
- career exploration readiness;
- subject/career interest patterns.

Aggregates must meet minimum-group and privacy thresholds before publication.

## 10. Psychometric governance

Before an assessment is marketed as a formal psychometric assessment, Secret Sharz should establish:
- construct definition;
- item blueprint;
- target population;
- scoring model;
- reliability evidence;
- validity evidence;
- bias/fairness review;
- language/cultural review;
- accessibility review;
- administration rules;
- interpretation rules;
- versioning;
- norm/reference strategy where applicable.

A newly created Secret Sharz assessment should initially be labelled as an exploratory/self-reflection tool until sufficient evidence supports stronger claims.

Proprietary assessment instruments must not be copied or recreated from copyrighted item banks.

## 11. Core product recommendation

The long-term Secret Sharz model is a Career Assessment Battery, not a single "career test":

Explore
→ Assess
→ Understand
→ Compare
→ Research
→ Plan
→ Act
→ Reassess

The same person can retake selected modules over time without creating a second career identity.
